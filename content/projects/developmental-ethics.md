# Teaching model behavior using ideas from developmental psychology

[Teaching Claude Why](https://www.anthropic.com/research/teaching-claude-why) motivates training models on reasons for appropriate behavior. Human studies of [explaining a story's moral](https://cognition.princeton.edu/publications/explaining-moral-story) and [moral-story framing](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24928424/) suggest concrete interventions to test, rather than treating human development as a loose analogy.

It would be useful to find which teaching methods help models generalize a principle to unfamiliar situations. Success in a human study does not establish that the same intervention will help a language model.

- Choose a specific finding about explanations, analogous cases or perspective-taking and translate it into matched training conditions.
- Control the amount and quality of training text so additional supervision is not mistaken for a special benefit of the method.
- Test actual behavior on unfamiliar situations, including conflicts between a principle and immediate reward.
- Check whether improvements survive subsequent fine-tuning.
