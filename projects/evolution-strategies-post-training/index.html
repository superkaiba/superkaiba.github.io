Evolutionary Algorithms as a Safer Alternative to Reinforcement Learning

Fleshed out

Heavy reinforcement learning seems to be the cause of most misalignment seen in current models (Denison et al., 2024; MacDiarmid et al., 2025; Qi et al., 2026). The alignment provided by reinforcement learning also seems to be quite shallow and easily removable (safety alignment mostly changes the first few output tokens: Qi et al., 2025; fine-tuning on 10 examples for under $0.20 removed GPT-3.5 Turbo's guardrails: Qi et al., 2024; DPO bypasses toxic capabilities without removing them: Lee et al., 2024; RL updates only 5% to 30% of parameters: Mukherjee et al., 2025). The capabilities community has developed evolutionary algorithms as an alternative to reinforcement learning (Salimans et al., 2017; Qiu et al., 2025; Sarkar et al., 2025), achieving competitive performance on capabilities benchmarks (Qiu et al., 2025; though GRPO stays marginally ahead on most tasks in Abdi et al., 2026) while being almost as efficient (evolutionary algorithms can be proven to be equivalent to RL with gaussian noise on the weights, i.e. a policy gradient over a distribution of parameters: Lehman et al., 2018).

These papers often present/discuss this robustness as an afterthought, because their goal is to improve capabilities and it doesn't really help with that (e.g. Qiu et al., 2025 list "less tendency to reward hacking" among their results: GRPO sometimes hacked a conciseness reward with short nonsense outputs, while ES never did; Lehman et al., 2018 show ES seeks parameters robust to perturbation, but leave the implications open), but I think it provides a solution to a lot of the problems the alignment community has been struggling with (shallow alignment, ruthless reward seeking, etc.), and merits further exploration from that perspective.
