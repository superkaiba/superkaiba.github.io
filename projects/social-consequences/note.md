# Teaching social behavior through experienced consequences

[SOTOPIA-π](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08715) improves agents' social goal completion through demonstrations and training on rated interactions. It also finds that LLM evaluators can overestimate the abilities of agents trained for these interactions, making independent evaluation important.

It would be useful to test what models learn when their own actions produce consequences within a social environment. For example, does repeatedly losing a partner's trust after misleading them teach more transferable behavior than receiving an instruction to be honest? Here, experiencing a consequence means receiving subsequent observations and outcomes from the environment.

- Build repeated interactions in which cooperation, misleading statements, and repairs affect later opportunities.
- Compare learning from direct instructions, descriptions of consequences, and consequences generated by the agent's own actions.
- Test transfer to new partners, settings, and incentives, including situations without an obvious evaluator.
- Evaluate actual effects on partners and long-term cooperation using independent checks, alongside ratings of how considerate the agent sounds.
