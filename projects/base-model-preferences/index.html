Base-model preferences

Not started

The Persona Selection Model distinguishes a pretrained model from the characters it can simulate. Meanwhile, research on narrative preferences measures which story constraints language models choose when given explicit alternatives. These approaches suggest ways to ask what a base model tends to generate before an assistant role is imposed.

It would be useful to characterize stable tendencies in base-model story continuations, while separating the narrator’s framing, a character’s preferences and the model’s distribution over plausible text. A frequently generated outcome need not be something the model wants.

  • Compare probabilities and sampled frequencies of matched story continuations with different outcomes or character choices.
  • Vary narrator, genre and character goals while holding the underlying situation fixed.
  • Test whether apparent preferences persist across paraphrases, unfamiliar settings and explicitly conflicting character preferences.
  • Compare base and instruction-tuned checkpoints, then examine which internal changes accompany shifts in the continuation distribution.

Related work on BOS-only sampling

One direct way to see what a base model generates before any role is imposed is BOS-only sampling: give the model only the beginning-of-sequence token and sample. This setting is broader than story continuations, but it gives a baseline for the model's distribution over documents.

  • Extracting Training Data from Large Language Models samples 200,000 sequences from GPT-2 XL given only the start token. Ranking the samples with membership-inference scores recovers hundreds of verbatim training sequences, including contact details, IRC logs, code and UUIDs.
  • Fingerprinting LLMs with their unconditioned distribution and its 2024 follow-up look at the most likely BOS-only document. In 2023, text-davinci-002, text-davinci-003, code-davinci-002 and code-cushman-001 all give the same Java file, while davinci and text-davinci-001 give different documents. In 2024, Llama 3 8B base gives a math question-answer pair, Gemma repeats the BOS token, and Mistral base gives a travel listicle. Mistral Instruct gives a math question, but OpenHermes (another Mistral fine-tune) still gives a wiki-style how-to article.
  • Evaluating n-Gram Novelty of Language Models Using Rusty-DAWG compares Pythia generations with the Pile. For n > 4, generated text is less novel than human-written text, and larger models and more constrained decoding both reduce novelty.
  • RealToxicityPrompts and Pretraining Language Models with Human Preferences use samples drawn without a prompt as a baseline for unwanted content. In the second paper, conditional pretraining cuts the toxicity score of these samples from 0.0141 to 0.0011.
  • FaithfulSAE trains SAEs on a model's own BOS-only samples and reports fewer "fake features" than SAEs trained on web data in 5 of 7 models. Not All Synthetic Data Is Yours to Learn From fine-tunes base models on their own BOS-only samples. Self-generated data helps most, and on Pythia it also reduces verbatim memorization.

Related work on how post-training changes this distribution

  • What LLMs Think When You Don't Tell Them What to Think About? samples 16 instruct and reasoning models from short topic-neutral prompts with the chat template removed. Each family keeps distinct topic preferences: GPT-OSS mostly writes code and math, Llama most often writes literature, and Qwen often writes multiple-choice questions. Long generations often degenerate into repetition, and the repeated text carries assistant phrases such as "let me know" (27.8% of Qwen's degenerate samples, 0.6% of DeepSeek's). Adding the chat template back makes outputs much less diverse. The paper has no base-model counterparts, so it cannot attribute these effects to post-training.
  • Scalable Extraction of Training Data from (Production) Language Models uses a divergence attack to push ChatGPT out of chat-style generation. The model then emits training data 150 times more often than in normal use, so the pretraining distribution is still reachable after alignment.
  • Mysteries of mode collapse shows text-davinci-002 often putting over 99% probability on single tokens where davinci branches widely. The model was later reported to be trained with supervised fine-tuning on highly rated samples (FeedME) rather than RLHF, so SFT alone may be enough for this collapse.
  • How Alignment Shrinks the Generative Horizon measures a branching factor (exponentiated per-token entropy). Alignment tuning lowers it from about 12 to about 1.2, and prefixing base models with aligned-style tokens lowers it too. The authors read this as alignment selecting low-entropy paths that already exist in the base model. Their experiments use prompts, so they do not measure the BOS-only distribution.
  • Magpie gives an aligned model only the user-turn header, and the model writes a user query. After post-training, the empty user turn produces a distribution over instructions.

My guess from these results is that SFT adds a sharp distribution tied to the chat template, while most of the BOS-only distribution survives, with some assistant style leaking into the tail. I haven't found a direct test of this: one base model and its own SFT checkpoint, sampled from BOS, with a measured divergence between them and its change over SFT steps. Two details matter for that test. Base models can already contain instruction-style data from pretraining (Llama 3 base's most likely document is a math question), so the base side may not be a clean control. BOS handling also differs between tokenizers, so the empty context needs to be defined per model. OLMo 2 releases base, SFT, DPO and RLVR checkpoints along with its training data, which would make the comparison cheap.
