First-, second-, and third-person identity in prompts

Fleshed out

TLDR: Test how "I am," "You are," and "The assistant is," placed in system or user prompts, affect the model's internal representations of itself and expressed traits/behaviors/identity

System prompts (or user prompts) often say things such as "You are Claude" or "You are a programmer" (second person pronouns) in order to confer a certain identity to the LLM assistant. However, based on the PSM (Marks et al., 2026), something that makes more sense is potentially saying "The assistant is Claude" or "The assistant is a programmer" (third person pronouns), "I am Claude" or "I am a programmer" (first person pronouns) or even "All AI assistants are programmers"

No one has conducted a principled study of the differences between these forms of giving the assistant its identity (the closest I could find is this paper which compares first person prompting in the chat template with third person prompting outside the chat template, but this is quite different; prior work on personas in system prompts varies which role is given, not how it is phrased: Zheng et al., 2024). This would be useful both to understand better how to instill a robust identity into a model with prompting/system prompting, and to gain better understanding of how the model represents the assistant (e.g. the Assistant Axis: Lu et al., 2026) and of the PSM in general.

Some questions to answer:

  • Which of these methods work best to prompt the assistant with an identity?
  • Are these represented the same way in activation space?
  • Does this change if it's in the system prompt or the user prompt?

First Steps

  • Vary prompting method for different identities/traits and in the system/user prompt and see what differences there are in expressed behavior and representation space, across multiple turns
