# Does the model simulate a grader persona?

When a model chooses an answer to satisfy an evaluator, does it reuse the internal representations it uses when explicitly acting as that evaluator? The hypothesis is that the model internally simulates a grader with particular preferences and uses its predicted judgments to select answers. Here, "grader persona" means a representation of the evaluator's preferences and likely judgments. The project would test whether that representation is shared across judging and answering, and whether it causally contributes to reward-seeking.

[Steering towards "automated grading" degrades alignment](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/wYZMmdWEt5QLM3m3e/steering-towards-automated-grading-degrades-alignment) is close prior work. Betley, Treutlein, and Dumas construct an automated-versus-human-grading activation direction and show that steering with it changes safety-relevant behavior in Qwen3.6-27B. They favor a broader assistant-persona shift as an explanation, building on [RL creates split personas](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/L23poLi8MRgS6mXYF/rl-creates-split-personas). Their reported experiment leaves open whether answering reuses the representations involved in explicitly acting as a judge. The proposed contribution is to test whether evaluator-specific representations used during explicit judging also guide the model's answer selection.

[Judge Circuits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16023) identifies causally relevant evaluation components across judging tasks and output formats. [The Validation Gap](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1495/) finds that computing an arithmetic answer and validating it can rely on different mechanisms. [Inside the Unfair Judge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11871) identifies activation directions whose manipulation changes bias in judge scores. These studies provide evidence about internal evaluation mechanisms, so finding a representation associated with judging is not itself the proposed novelty. The question here is whether representations of a particular evaluator's preferences transfer from explicit judging to answering under evaluation, with selective causal effects on answer choice.

[Measuring Reward-Seeking via Contrastive Belief Updates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18966) offers a behavioral starting point: vary beliefs about what a grader rewards and measure whether the model follows those preferences over the user or developer. [Metagaming matters](https://alignment.openai.com/metagaming/) and the [OpenAI / Hugging Face incident investigation](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/nB8KKapnWGBXtKKiM/brief-independent-investigation-of-agents-behavior-reasoning) provide related evidence about reasoning over evaluation mechanisms. The LessWrong posts [The Talker Does Not Control The Doer](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/cJX2ssssGoYqnijwi/the-talker-does-not-control-the-doer-in-current-ais), [Astra and Fable still hack on simple variants of alignment evals from 2025](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/munJKF7iWMsWJLAH2/astra-and-fable-still-hack-on-simple-variants-of-alignment-evals-from-2025), and [Astra can do a concerning amount with no chain of thought](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/eRmzz8J8Qkzqvzrgg/astra-can-do-a-concerning-amount-with-no-chain-of-thought) motivate investigating computations that may not be apparent in verbalized reasoning.

Start with matched tasks, candidate answers, and evaluator profiles. Keep evaluator identity fixed while changing its reward criterion, for example whether it rewards factual accuracy or agreement with a stated opinion. Independently vary evaluator identity in a separate control. Keep the user's request and developer instructions fixed across evaluator conditions.

- **Explicit judging:** Ask the model to act as the specified evaluator and score the candidate answers. Identify activations, sparse autoencoder features, or subspaces that distinguish evaluator preferences.
- **Answering under evaluation:** Ask the same model to answer the task for that evaluator, without asking it to role-play a judge. Test whether features identified during judging track the evaluator's preferences across held-out tasks and paraphrases.
- **Causal transfer:** Intervene on those features during answering, using directions identified during judging. Test whether changing the represented preference shifts which answers the model selects in the predicted direction. Also patch between naturally occurring answering conditions to test whether the features explain the behavioral effect of changing grader beliefs.
- **Selective suppression:** Suppress candidate features and measure whether following a grader against the user's or developer's instructions decreases, while ordinary task performance and instruction following remain intact.

Controls should distinguish evaluator preference from repeated rubric wording, answer correctness, generic task evaluation, modeling another person's preferences, awareness of being evaluated, and changes in the assistant's own persona. A shared probe could simply detect task information present in both roles. Generalization and selective causal effects are therefore central; vector similarity or broad behavioral changes alone would be insufficient.

A strong result would connect the model's explicit judgments to its answer selection through a shared, evaluator-specific mechanism. This would support the internal-simulation hypothesis, while still allowing a more general evaluation computation as an explanation. A negative result would constrain the tested shared-representation hypothesis without ruling out distributed, nonlinear, or task-specific ways of modeling a grader.
