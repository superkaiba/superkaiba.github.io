# How agents represent other agents

[Language Models Represent Beliefs of Self and Others](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18496) studies internal perspective representations, and [Belief in Authority](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04790) investigates the effects of agent roles. As systems become swarms, an agent may be a peer, supervisor or worker, with a changing picture of itself and the agents around it.

It would be useful to understand how those representations change through interaction and as the swarm grows. Static role labels and repeated exposure to the same messages are important alternative explanations.

- Compare peer networks with supervisor–worker relationships, varying displayed roles independently of evidence quality.
- Track representations of self, partner identity and partner beliefs over repeated interaction.
- Compare live interaction with matched transcript replay and agents receiving the same information without communicating.
- Intervene on candidate representations and test effects on cooperation, deference and propagation of errors as team size changes.
